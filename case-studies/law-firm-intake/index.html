Illustrative example

An intake agent for a regional law firm

Not a client result · Legal services

Typical outcome Intake qualifies, routes, and follows up on its own, instead of taking two days a week of staff time.

The challenge

Picture a regional law firm running new-client intake by hand. Every inbound lead — phone, web form, referral — lands in a shared inbox. A paralegal triages each one, decides whether it fits the firm's practice areas, gathers the missing information over email, and routes it to the right attorney. Follow-up is manual; if a prospect goes quiet for a few days, nobody notices.

In this example, slow response and dropped follow-up are where qualified leads fall out. Intake is taking about two days a week of senior staff time that should be going to billable matters.

Our approach

The first session is spent with the person who actually handles intake, watching the workflow as it happens — not the documented one. Most of the value shows up there: a handful of decisions made on instinct that nobody has written down.

From that, intake splits into three jobs: capture (get the inbound lead into a structured record), qualify (decide whether the matter fits the firm), and route (hand it to the right attorney with the right context). An agent takes each job. A person reviews it where the cost of a mistake is high, and nothing is sent until someone on the firm's side says so.

What we built

  • A single intake form that replaces three different web entry points and pre-fills based on referral source.
  • An agent that scores each new matter against the firm's practice criteria, shows the reasoning, and stops for a person before anything is sent.
  • Routing into the firm's existing case management system, including a conflict-check trigger and a calendar hold for the assigned attorney. A person confirms the handoff.
  • A follow-up agent that drafts a note for prospects who go quiet, in the firm's voice, and waits for approval before it is sent. It stops the moment a human replies.
  • A weekly digest for the person who owns intake, showing volume, qualification rate, time-to-first-response, and any matters that fell out of the pipeline.

Typical outcome

  • Typical outcome: about 12 hours a week of paralegal and attorney time back on billable work.
  • Typical outcome: most new leads get a first response in minutes, not hours.
  • Typical outcome: a higher share of qualified leads make it to a consult.
  • Typical outcome: qualified matters stop falling out of the pipeline.

Timeline

  • Week 1: Workflow shadowing, scope agreement, and shared documentation of the qualification rules.
  • Weeks 2–3: Build of the intake form, qualification model, and routing into the case management system.
  • Week 4: Pilot across the practice groups, follow-up sequences live, weekly digest delivered.
