Illustrative example

An onboarding agent for an e-commerce brand

Not a client result · E-commerce

Typical outcome Onboarding is mapped in one session and running soon after, without anyone having to keep touching it.

The challenge

Picture a DTC brand that has grown faster than its onboarding process. New customers hit a sequence of touchpoints — welcome email, account setup nudge, first-purchase offer, post-purchase confirmation, review request — but every step lives in a different tool, is triggered by a different person, and breaks quietly during sale weekends.

The aim is one agent, not five handoffs, that keeps running on a busy weekend without anyone babysitting it. A message to a customer waits for a person to approve it before it is sent.

Our approach

One working session. A marker on a whiteboard, the existing onboarding walked step by step, and a circle around every place the process depends on a person remembering to do something. In this example there are seven of those.

From that session the target state is clear: a single orchestrated workflow with one owner, every handoff explicit, and the failure modes monitored.

What we built

  • An agent that starts from a new order in the e-commerce platform and runs every downstream step from there.
  • The agent works across the brand's existing email, SMS, reviews, and helpdesk tools. Nothing has to move to a new platform, and a customer-facing message waits for approval.
  • Branching logic for first-time vs. returning customers, and for orders that include items requiring extra setup guidance.
  • A monitor that watches for any onboarding step that fails or stalls, and tells the on-call operator before customers notice.
  • A simple weekly report showing onboarding completion rate, drop-off points, and time-from-purchase-to-first-engagement.

Typical outcome

  • Typical outcome: less time from purchase to a fully activated customer.
  • Typical outcome: more new customers finish every onboarding step.
  • Typical outcome: the workflow keeps running on a busy weekend without anyone babysitting it.
  • Typical outcome: the weekly manual handoff checklist goes away.

Timeline

  • Day 1: Whiteboard session, end-state agreed, scope locked.
  • Days 2–5: Build of the orchestration, integrations into the existing tool stack, branching logic for customer types.
  • Day 6: Side-by-side test against the manual workflow on a sample of real orders.
  • Day 7: Cutover. Monitoring live. Manual checklist retired.
