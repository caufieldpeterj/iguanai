What we actually build

IguanAI Team · March 24, 2026

It is tempting, in a category as broad as AI, to position a firm as able to build anything. We have gone the other way. Most of what we do for executives in the first year of a relationship falls into three kinds of agent, and saying so plainly has made it easier for everyone to scope work. We build them primarily on Grok Bot, and we choose the platform for the client.

The first is an agent for intake and follow-up. These are the operations a team executes the same way every week. Client intake. Onboarding paperwork. Invoice reconciliation. Report distribution. The work is mapping what actually happens — as distinct from what the org chart says happens — and then giving an agent the job. A person still approves anything that gets sent or spent. The payoff is not always speed. More often it is that the process becomes reliable, and the best people stop spending their best hours on it.

The second is an agent that works across the tools the business already uses: email, calendar, documents, and the systems the work lives in. It knows a specific body of knowledge — products, policies, client history — and it does a job with that knowledge. It can take the first layer of repetitive questions, or give a client a better front door than a contact form. The critical design choice is scope. An agent that tries to do everything is brittle. An agent that does one job well is durable. If it cannot finish without a person, it stops and asks.

The third is a scheduled monitor that reports on its own. This sounds mundane, and it is the engagement that most often surprises people with how much changes. The work is part data, part editorial: identifying the handful of numbers that actually tell you how the business is doing, pulling them from the systems where they live, and having a monitor report them on a clock, in a form you can read in sixty seconds in the morning. The hardest conversation is almost always about which numbers matter. Once that is settled, the build is fast.

Under all three is the same pattern. Pick something specific. Deliver something working. A person approves what goes out. Document it so someone else can own it. Repeat on the next thing. The firms that have told us they wish they had started earlier are uniformly the ones who resisted the urge to build a platform before they had built an agent.

— The IguanAI Team
